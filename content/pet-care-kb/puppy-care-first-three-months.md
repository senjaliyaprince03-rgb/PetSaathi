# Comprehensive Puppy Care Guide: First 3 Months in Indian Homes

Welcoming a puppy (8 to 12 weeks old) into an Indian apartment or residential society requires structured schedules for deworming, early socialization, balanced nutrition, and teething management.

## 1. Feeding & Nutrition (Weeks 8 to 12)

- **Feeding Frequency**: 4 meals per day until 12 weeks of age, then transition to 3 meals per day until 6 months.
- **Dietary Composition**:
  - High-protein starter kibble soaked in warm water or bone broth.
  - Plain curd (dahi) at room temperature provides natural probiotics for fragile puppy gut biomes.
  - Boiled shredded chicken and soft pumpkin puree; avoid onions, garlic, excess salt, and spices.
  - Never feed cow's milk directly, as most weaned puppies are lactose intolerant and develop acute diarrhea.

## 2. Deworming Protocol

Puppies in tropical Indian environments carry high burdens of roundworms, hookworms, and tapeworms transmitted from mother or soil:
- **Frequency**: Deworm every 2 weeks until 12 weeks of age, then once monthly until 6 months.
- **Medication**: Pediatric suspension (e.g. Pyrantel Pamoate + Febantel) dosed strictly by body weight under veterinary prescription.
- Always monitor stool post-deworming for shed parasites.

## 3. Quarantine & Society Ground Safety

- **No Public Ground Walks**: Keep the puppy indoors off society compound lawns, pavements, and public parks until **14 days after the final DHPPi booster** (around 16 weeks).
- **Parvovirus & Distemper Hazard**: Canine parvovirus remains infectious in society soil and grass for up to 12 months. Do not permit unvaccinated puppies to walk where stray dogs or unknown pets roam.
- **Early Indoor Socialization**: Introduce household sounds (mixers, doorbells, vacuum cleaners) and invite vaccinated healthy adult family dogs for controlled indoor play.

## 4. Teething & Destructive Chewing

- Puppies begin losing deciduous baby teeth between 12 to 16 weeks.
- Provide frozen wet washcloths or frozen carrot sticks to soothe inflamed gums.
- Use safe non-toxic rubber chew toys (Kong style) filled with frozen curd or peanut butter (xylitol-free).
