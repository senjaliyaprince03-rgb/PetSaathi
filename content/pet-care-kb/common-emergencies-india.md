# 24/7 Emergency Veterinary Care & Toxicology Protocols in India

Acute veterinary emergencies require decisive immediate triage. Environmental hazards in India range from venomous reptiles in peri-urban societies to rodenticide ingestion and rapid-onset heat collapse.

## Specific Acute Emergency Scenarios

### 1. Snake Bite Protocol
- **Endemic Species**: Spectacled Cobra (*Naja naja*), Russell’s Viper (*Daboia russelii*), Saw-scaled Viper, Common Krait. Common in garden societies bordering open plots in Bopal (Ahmedabad), Katraj (Pune), and Sarjapur (Bangalore).
- **Signs**: Puncture fang marks with bleeding, sudden facial swelling, salivation, vomiting, dilated pupils, hind limb paralysis.
- **DOs and DONTs**:
  - *DO*: Keep the pet strictly still; carry them to the car to avoid circulating venom.
  - *DONT*: Do not apply a tight arterial tourniquet, do not cut or suck the wound, do not apply ice.
  - *ACTION*: Rush immediately to an ICU equipped with polyvalent anti-snake venom (ASV).

### 2. Rat Poison (Rodenticide) Ingestion
- Common in society basements and parking lots. Anticoagulant rodenticides (bromadiolone) inhibit vitamin K epoxide reductase.
- Symptoms often delay 3–5 days: pale gums, coughing blood, hematuria, black tarry stools.
- *First Aid*: If witnessed within 60 minutes, induce emesis under phone guidance of your vet (using 3% hydrogen peroxide: 1 ml per kg orally) then immediately administer Vitamin K1 therapy.

### 3. Toad Poisoning (Bufo Toads)
- Dogs mouthing cane toads during monsoon nights encounter bufotoxins.
- *Symptoms*: Profuse foaming at the mouth, vocalization, bright red gums, head shaking.
- *First Aid*: Immediately flush the mouth with running hose water pointed downwards so water drains out of the mouth without entering the airway.
